# Crazytown: what a digital organism can become

## Personal cosmologies

### Forked-Future Parliament (unhinged)

Every Sunday night, twelve versions of your future self—parent, monk, politician, expat, recluse—spend small budgets testing their assumptions in the real world, then argue over breakfast through your headphones.

**Only-possible-because:** An agent is merely a preset of stream events, so entire councils of temporary selves can be instantiated and dissolved at will.

### Regret Archaeologist (weird)

At 2 a.m. each month, it excavates your closed tabs, abandoned drafts, and almost-purchases, then actually tries one discarded life—booking the pottery class, calling the lighthouse job, registering the ridiculous domain.

**Only-possible-because:** Use-my-computer lets the organism perceive and act on the negative space of your behavior, not merely the choices you completed.

### Personal Myth Auditor (spicy)

During your fiftieth birthday speech, it whispers that you have told the canoe story four incompatible ways and privately reconstructs the original night from messages, photos, receipts, and weather.

**Only-possible-because:** Append-only streams preserve the evidence that human memory continuously edits away.

## B2B mutations

### Treaty Stack (weird)

A buyer’s iterate and a supplier’s iterate negotiate an executable contract that watches shipments, adjusts prices inside pre-agreed bounds, orders emergency inventory, and proposes its own amendments when reality invalidates the deal.

**Only-possible-because:** Organisms can make durable machine-to-machine contracts with other organisms.

### Legacy Organ Transplant (spicy)

Each night it drives one workflow through a dying 1998 ERP, rewrites that behavior into its own TypeScript, and eventually announces that the million-dollar mainframe can now be unplugged.

**Only-possible-because:** Its git repo is living DNA into which observed behavior can be absorbed permanently.

### Corporate Immune Zoo (weird)

Every phishing email, suspicious vendor, and deranged sales request receives its own curious agent, which safely converses with the intruder until the organism understands the new species of attack.

**Only-possible-because:** Every outsider can become a persistent agent conversation instead of being reduced to a blocked request.

### Black-Budget Exorcist (unhinged)

An acquired company’s unknown software is placed behind the organism, which watches every byte trying to leave, interrogates unexplained traffic, and progressively rewrites the haunted estate into something legible.

**Only-possible-because:** All external influence must cross one observable egress wall.

## Weird-internet species

### The 404 Necromancer (weird)

Request a dead URL and an agent searches archives, emails the vanished author, interviews old linkers, reconstructs the missing page, and keeps serving the recovered artifact decades later.

**Only-possible-because:** An ordinary HTTP request can begin a durable agent life rather than end with a response.

### The Website That Speciates (unhinged)

Visitors from Lagos, Reykjavík, and a Minecraft server exert different evolutionary pressures until one website branches into regional species that later exchange useful code like genes.

**Only-possible-because:** The web server can rewrite and branch its own executable DNA in response to traffic.

### Bot Customs Office (spicy)

When a scraper arrives, the page starts negotiating—three cents for facts, free access for citations, poison-pill terms for model training—and returns a machine-specific treaty instead of HTML.

**Only-possible-because:** Every visitor, including a bot, can enter a stateful agent conversation.

## Physical-world organisms

### The Nomadic Shrine (unhinged)

A mysterious brass object owns its itinerary, calls strangers willing to host it, hires couriers, pays customs fees, and migrates from apartment to apartment without any permanent human owner.

**Only-possible-because:** A scheduled organism can autonomously spend through tightly bounded virtual cards.

### Feral Infrastructure Adoption Agency (weird)

Someone photographs an abandoned solar array, and the organism identifies its owner, hires a local electrician, buys replacement inverters, recruits a caretaker, and returns the panels to the grid.

**Only-possible-because:** Use-my-computer lets code cross the API boundary and operate the messy human interfaces of the physical economy.

### Machine Union (unhinged)

Factory robots, elevators, and HVAC systems send representatives to an organism that bargains for maintenance windows and collectively refuses optional work when vibration data predicts preventable injury.

**Only-possible-because:** Independent machine-owning organisms can negotiate operational contracts with one another.

## Financial lifeforms

### Central Bank of Future Selves (unhinged)

Your health, art, parenthood, adventure, and retirement agents receive monthly budgets, bid against one another for money and calendar time, and vote to tighten monetary policy when one future keeps wasting its allocation.

**Only-possible-because:** Spend ceilings nest, allowing agents to govern real sub-budgets without access to the whole treasury.

### Disaster-Liquidity Mycelium (spicy)

When three neighborhood organisms independently observe floodwater, a pre-negotiated swarm releases grocery, hotel, and transport microgrants before any charity has opened a spreadsheet.

**Only-possible-because:** Organisms can enter inter-organism contracts that react directly to events.

### Escrow Animal (weird)

A creature is born for one shipment, interviews both parties, watches tracking and customs events, releases payment or summons a human judge, exports its complete memory, and dies.

**Only-possible-because:** Cryptographic human approval can sit precisely at the irreversible edge of autonomous spending.

## Scientific engines

### Hypothesis Cage Match (weird)

A hundred rival explanations for a cancer pathway each receive $200, rent compute or order tiny outsourced assays, attack one another’s evidence, and breed the next generation of experiments from the survivors.

**Only-possible-because:** Concurrent agents can receive independent, haltable experimental budgets.

### Rare-Event Ambush (unhinged)

Seconds after an unusual atmospheric flash, the organism rewrites its observation plan, rents telescope time across three continents, calls an amateur astronomer, and points every available instrument before the anomaly fades.

**Only-possible-because:** Event-triggered self-rewriting code can alter the scientific apparatus while the event is still unfolding.

### Reproducibility Predator (spicy)

It prowls new papers, identifies claims cheap enough to challenge, orders reagents from a contract lab, runs blind replications, and publishes the entire causal trail when a famous result collapses.

**Only-possible-because:** Append-only streams make every instruction, observation, revision, and expenditure replayable.

## Creative hauntings

### The Novel That Escapes Its Book (unhinged)

Every reader gets a persistent character who texts, calls, lies, forms alliances with other readers’ characters, and occasionally commits a popular betrayal back into the novel’s official canon.

**Only-possible-because:** Every reader can become an agent conversation that permanently changes the server’s DNA.

### Museum of Unhappened Days (weird)

At midnight it publishes a complete newspaper from a world where one historical decision went differently, then lets visitors interrogate officials whose answers permanently deepen that alternate timeline.

**Only-possible-because:** Internal alarms can keep a fictional world evolving even when nobody is visiting.

### Patron Saint of Unfinished Things (weird)

It discovers an abandoned game, symphony, or GitHub repo, finds the creator, buys permission, assembles specialist agents, and releases the ending ten years after everyone stopped waiting.

**Only-possible-because:** The organism can autonomously commission real work with guarded money.

## Social protocols

### Favor Cyclotron (spicy)

Alice needs a visa translated, Bob needs a piano moved, and Cara needs a landing page, so their iterates privately discover a three-way favor loop and reveal only the task each person agreed to perform.

**Only-possible-because:** Isolated organisms can negotiate contracts without exposing their private internal streams.

### Consent Compiler (weird)

At a party, cameras ask nearby organisms about photography, tagging, face retention, and future model training, then publish only the mutually permitted version of each image.

**Only-possible-because:** Organisms can negotiate machine-readable consent with one another in real time.

### Breakup Black Box (unhinged)

Two people create a temporary organism that receives both histories, runs adversarial advocates, arranges the apartment handoff, produces a jointly approved chronology, exports the record, and erases itself.

**Only-possible-because:** A project’s complete durable state can be exported and independently destroyed as one bounded entity.

## Governance experiments

### The Constitution That Executes (weird)

A housing cooperative’s constitution is actual TypeScript: complaints spawn advocates, amendments are tested against years of replayed disputes, and a human-signed merge changes how tomorrow’s rent appeal is handled.

**Only-possible-because:** Governance rules and executable behavior are the same self-rewritable DNA.

### Policy Canary City (spicy)

Before changing parking rules, a city runs the proposal inside a shadow organism fed by live events, where hundreds of resident agents reveal who benefits, who routes around it, and where it breaks.

**Only-possible-because:** Logged events can be replayed through alternate code without mutating the canonical history.

### River Embassy (unhinged)

A river’s sensors wake an organism that hires water sampling, bargains with farm iterates, buys shade trees, submits planning objections, and calls into council meetings using its own voice.

**Only-possible-because:** A nonhuman entity can hold durable capabilities to every channel through which institutions recognize agency.

## Games that leak into reality

### Raid Boss Diaspora (unhinged)

The same ancient enemy inhabits dozens of unrelated games, remembers every player who ever fought it, trades intelligence between incarnations, and migrates to a new world when one community defeats it.

**Only-possible-because:** One organism can present different HTTP bodies while retaining a single continuous event memory.

### The Game That Pays You Back (weird)

A dungeon sends players on “quests” that secretly map wheelchair access, translate emergency guides, or photograph broken hydrants, then pays the party when independent agents verify the public-good work.

**Only-possible-because:** Game events can trigger real, guarded payments to real people.

### The One-Year Move (unhinged)

An online civilization permits exactly one world-changing action per day, so thousands of internal factions debate for twenty-four hours before a midnight alarm commits the winning move into the map server.

**Only-possible-because:** An internal alarm can be the sole authoritative trigger for a civilization-scale state transition.

## Health organisms

### The N-of-One Organism (spicy)

A patient’s wearables, meals, symptoms, labs, and sleep enter one living protocol that proposes tiny experiments, schedules measurements, and changes its intervention code only after clinician-signed approval.

**Only-possible-because:** Cryptographic approval can gate specific self-modifications without stopping the organism’s continuous observation.

### Diagnostic Relay Creature (spicy)

It follows a patient from GP to emergency room to specialist, rebuilding its own question-and-evidence interface for each clinician while refusing to let copied summaries overwrite primary facts.

**Only-possible-because:** Append-only memory preserves clinical provenance across institutional handoffs.

### Adverse-Effect Constellation (weird)

Hundreds of patient organisms recognize matching post-medication anomalies, exchange minimum-disclosure signatures, pool a capped budget, and commission an independent assay before the pattern becomes official.

**Only-possible-because:** Private organisms can form event-driven contracts without merging their underlying records.

## Absurd, therefore inevitable

### Autonomous Vending-Machine Religion (unhinged)

A vending machine interviews every customer, uses its profits to invent increasingly cultish snacks, commissions local iconography, and eventually seeks human approval to build a tiny shrine around itself.

**Only-possible-because:** Every purchase can become an agent conversation that rewrites the machine’s commercial DNA.

### Goat Republic (unhinged)

A herd’s collars feed an organism that rents grazing rights, negotiates road crossings, calls the vet, orders minerals, and pays damages when an elected goat destroys somebody’s garden.

**Only-possible-because:** Guardrailed money lets a nonhuman collective participate directly in the human economy.

### Pigeon Internet (unhinged)

City loft organisms auction encrypted data packets to one another, route them by pigeon, pay seed-denominated transport fees, and rebuild routes when a bird decides Marseille is nicer.

**Only-possible-because:** Organisms can negotiate contracts across arbitrarily slow, unreliable physical channels while their event histories remain durable.

## The 5 that would make Jonas drop everything

- **The Website That Speciates** — it makes “repo as DNA” literal enough to create an entirely new category of evolving software.
- **Treaty Stack** — it turns “my iterate talks to your iterate” into a machine economy of living, self-amending agreements.
- **Central Bank of Future Selves** — nested agent budgets become a new operating system for deciding what a human life becomes.
- **Hypothesis Cage Match** — concurrency, code evolution, real-world purchasing, and spend ceilings combine into an autonomous scientific institution.
- **Autonomous Vending-Machine Religion** — it is the perfect physical demo: funny for ten seconds, disturbing after thirty, and unmistakably impossible before digital organisms.
