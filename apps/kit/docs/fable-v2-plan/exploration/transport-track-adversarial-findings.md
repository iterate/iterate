# Transport-track adversarial findings (three lenses, 2026-07-31)

Extracted verbatim from the adversarial review workflow. Fixes applied to PLAN.md's Transport track same day.

## lens

**Overall:** The track's foundation survives adversarial live verification: the SFU + WebSocket-adapter architecture works as described, the adapter really does dial arbitrary wss endpoints with capability-URL-style auth, TURN-over-TLS on 443 is real for the esp blob, and 'the SFU is just another WebSocket client of the /pcm DO' remains an accurate framing. But the track is written as if the SFU leg were stateless and free, and it is neither: 30-second track/session inactivity timeouts plus per-GB egress pricing at GA force an SFU-leg lifecycle design (the D8-equivalent state machine) that the plan simply does not have — that is the one blocker. Beyond it, the plan overstates what RTP inherits from D7 (timestamp echo and in-band end-of-turn both do NOT exist on the WebRTC lane, weakening requirement 9 and resurrecting the PTT-release race), treats adapter reconnect as symmetric when the ingest side has none, omits the stereo-fixed PCM format and the channel-conversion work it forces, presents the TURN/TLS rung as stack-independent when libpeer lacks it, and measures steady-state latency while ignoring the session-establishment and post-outage recovery latencies that are the WS lane's strongest remaining advantage. All of these are fixable with plan-text edits and additional W1/W3 measurement items before execution starts; none invalidates the decided direction.

### [BLOCKER] The track has no SFU-leg session-economics design, and the live limits make one mandatory: SFU tracks are garbage-collected after 30 s without media packets, and sessions/tracks are reusable for only 30 s after connectivity loss. A sleeping/PTT-idle device therefore loses its mic track, the egress adapter bound to it (adapter is created against sessionId+trackName), and — beyond 30 s — the whole session, forcing full re-setup per utterance. The alternative (keep the leg warm with continuous audio) is billable at GA: the adapter's fixed 48 kHz stereo PCM egress is 0.69 GB/h ≈ $0.035/h ≈ $25/month per always-on device; two always-on devices ≈ the entire 1,000 GB/month account free tier. D8's 2 s mic-ring dial-masking also silently assumes the SFU leg is already up — mic audio only reaches the DO while the egress adapter+track are live. The plan defines states for the Grok session (D8) but none for the SFU session/tracks/adapters, and its 'pricing at GA' risk line carries no numbers and no design response.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:352-406 (Transport track, no SFU lifecycle states), PLAN.md:186-190 (2 s mic ring), PLAN.md:403-406 (risk line); https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/limits/ ('Tracks will automatically timeout and be garbage collected after 30 seconds of inactivity'; 'Sessions and tracks will remain available for reuse for 30 seconds before timing out'); https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/media-transport-adapters/websocket-adapter/ ($0.05/GB egress post-GA, counts toward free tier)
- Proposed fix: Add to the Transport track a 'SFU leg lifecycle' subsection mirroring D8's state machine: when the WebRTC session + two adapters are created/kept-warm/torn down (per-boot vs per-conversation), what keeps the mic track alive across silences (Opus DTX vs comfort frames vs accept per-utterance re-setup), who re-creates adapters after GC, and where the D8 mic ring lives when the leg is cold. Add to W1's measurement list: (a) whether DTX/silence keeps a track alive and what the egress adapter emits during it, (b) steady-state egress GB/h per device, (c) cost of the chosen warm policy at GA prices. Replace the bare 'pricing at GA' risk with the concrete numbers ($0.035/h/device stream-adapter egress at GA; free tier = ~1,000 device-warm-hours/month).

### [SERIOUS] D7's scope amendment ('on the WebRTC lane, RTP provides sequence numbers and timestamps natively, so this header's whole job is done by the transport there') and requirement row 9 ('WebRTC lane: RTP timestamps native') are wrong on both of D7's jobs. Job 1, timestamp echo, is a cross-direction mechanism (device tags each mic frame with the timestamp of the speaker audio playing at that instant); the adapter's protobuf timestamps are single-track SFU-side stamps, and the device's actual playback delay is hidden inside the closed blob's ~100 ms adaptive jitter buffer — so server-side AEC alignment on the WebRTC lane is tens-of-ms uncertain vs the ±0.5 ms rig gate, i.e. requirement 9 is materially weaker on this lane, not 'native'. Job 2, the in-band end-of-turn frame, has no WebRTC equivalent at all: WebRTC guarantees no ordering across RTP, the data channel, and the control lane, so the PTT-release-races-the-last-frames problem D7 was built to kill returns on the target lane.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:166-170 (D7 scope amendment), PLAN.md:396-398 (W3 'RTP gives us D7's sequence+timestamp machinery natively'), PLAN.md:479 (requirement row 9); https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/media-transport-adapters/websocket-adapter/ (Packet {sequenceNumber, timestamp, payload}, stream mode only, single track); exploration/webrtc-esp-side.md §3 (blob jitter buffer default 100 ms, RTCP not exposed)
- Proposed fix: Rewrite the D7 amendment to: 'RTP replaces the header's uplink sequence/timestamp fields as observed at the DO, but NOT timestamp echo (needs a device-side report of playback position — data-channel or control-lane message, designed in W2/W3) and NOT the end-of-turn frame (no cross-stream ordering exists in WebRTC; end-of-turn on this lane is a worker-side drain protocol: control-lane release signal + drain window keyed on the egress adapter's seq/timestamps, or xAI server-VAD).' Change requirement row 9's WebRTC cell from 'RTP timestamps native' to 'alignment mechanism TBD in W2/W3; server-side AEC is weaker on this lane until it exists'. Add both to W3's rig scenarios.

### [SERIOUS] The plan treats 'adapter auto-reconnect' as a single lane property, but auto-reconnect exists for Stream (egress) mode ONLY; the ingest (downlink) adapter has no reconnect at all. After any 5 s window (stream) or immediately (ingest), the adapter closes permanently and must be recreated by API call — and recreating an ingest adapter mints a NEW auto-generated session id, so the device must re-pull the downlink track (tracks/new + renegotiate over the control lane) before it hears anything again. A DO restart/deploy mid-conversation therefore costs a multi-second downlink outage plus a signaling round-trip, not a 'best-effort gap'. Nothing in the plan assigns a watchdog to notice closed adapters and re-drive this.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:379-381 (W1 'characterize the adapter's beta edges (auto-reconnect is documented best-effort, not gapless)'), PLAN.md:403-405 (risks); https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-05-29-websocket-adapter-auto-reconnect/ ('Stream mode (egress)… If the endpoint remains unavailable after the 5-second reconnect window, the adapter closes and must be recreated'); adapter doc FAQ ('each instance is unidirectional'; ingest 'a fresh session is auto-generated')
- Proposed fix: In W1, split the characterization into per-direction items: (a) stream-adapter reconnect gap measurement across a forced DO restart; (b) ingest-adapter death → full recreate cost (API + track re-pull + renegotiate through the control lane), measured. In W3 add: worker-side adapter watchdog (closed-adapter detection → recreate → event record), and a rig scenario 'DO redeploy mid-utterance' asserting both directions recover within a stated budget. Amend the risk line from 'reconnect gaps' to 'stream-side 5 s best-effort reconnect; ingest side none — recreate + renegotiate'.

### [SERIOUS] The adapter's PCM format is 16-bit s16le 48 kHz STEREO (left/right interleaved), fixed at creation, both directions — the plan never says stereo, and W1's 'xAI accepts 48 kHz natively so possibly zero resampling' is wrong as written: channel conversion is mandatory in both directions (adapter stereo → xAI mono uplink; xAI mono → duplicated-stereo ingest downlink; xAI's session config has rate but no channel parameter), and stereo doubles the billable egress bandwidth (0.69 GB/h instead of 0.35). Only the sample-RATE conversion can be avoided, and only by pinning the xAI session to 48 kHz instead of its 24 kHz default.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:375-378 (W1 'accept the adapter's 48 kHz s16le PCM protobuf frames; xAI accepts 48 kHz natively so possibly zero resampling'); https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/media-transport-adapters/websocket-adapter/ ('Stereo (left/right interleaved)', 'audio format is fixed at creation time'); https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/audio/voice-agent (audio.input/output.format has type+rate only, no channels; 24 kHz default)
- Proposed fix: Correct W1 to: 'accept the adapter's 48 kHz s16le STEREO PCM protobuf frames; DO downmixes stereo→mono uplink and duplicates mono→stereo on ingest; pin the xAI session to 48 kHz to avoid rate conversion (channel conversion is unavoidable)'. Note the stereo-doubled egress in the cost line (feeds the blocker finding's numbers).

### [SERIOUS] Barge-in through the SFU path is unimplementable unless the DO stays the pacing authority, and the plan doesn't say so. The ingest adapter is created in 'buffer' mode with undocumented pacing and NO documented flush/cancel primitive; the device-side jitter buffer is inside the closed blob, also unflushable. xAI delivers assistant audio faster than realtime, so if the DO forwards bursts straight into the adapter, seconds of already-committed audio sit beyond reach when the user barges in — the current lane's epoch-purge/flush-fence machinery has no equivalent on either SFU leg. W3 schedules a 'barge-in latency through the SFU path' measurement but no design rule that makes the measurement passable.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:393-398 (W3 barge-in scenario, no pacing rule), PLAN.md:184-186 (D8 unchanged claim); adapter doc (ingest 'mode': 'buffer', 32 KB/message, no flush API documented, 'send audio in small, frequent chunks'); exploration/webrtc-esp-side.md §3 (blob jitter buffer, no exposed flush)
- Proposed fix: Add a design rule to W1/W3: the DO buffers all provider audio itself and feeds the ingest adapter at ~realtime with a bounded lead (target ≤200 ms in flight beyond the DO), so barge-in cancellation truncates the DO-side queue; the in-flight lead bounds worst-case barge-in overhang and is asserted by the W3 rig scenario. Add 'characterize ingest buffer-mode pacing and whether over-feeding is possible' to W1's measurement list.

### [SERIOUS] There is no ICE restart on the SFU (docs' recovery advice is 'establish a new session'; /renegotiate exists only for track add/remove), so any outage beyond the 30 s reuse window costs a full re-setup — ≥2 control-lane SDP round-trips (initial offer/answer, then the downlink pull which returns an SFU offer requiring a PUT /renegotiate answer), 4-6 worker HTTPS calls, ICE checks, DTLS handshake, and two adapter creations — plausibly 1.5-4 s. W1 measures only steady-state media latency; nothing measures session-establishment or post-outage recovery time against the WS lane's reconnect ladder (which is one TLS+WS handshake), and the plan never states whether the WS PCM lane is kept warm as an instant fallback while the WebRTC leg re-establishes.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:375-381 (W1 measures 'end-to-end latency vs the current lane' only), PLAN.md:393-398 (W3 ladder has no recovery-latency budget); https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/limits/ ('Establishing a new session after connectivity is restored'; 30 s reuse window); https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/https-api/ (pull flow: tracks/new returns Offer → PUT /renegotiate with Answer)
- Proposed fix: Add to W1: measure cold session-establishment time (signaling + ICE + DTLS + both adapters) and post-outage recovery time (< 30 s and > 30 s cases) alongside media latency; both numbers go in the done-list gate. Add to W3: state the fallback policy explicitly — WS PCM lane stays connected (warm) while WebRTC is primary, with a stated switchover budget, or accept the measured re-setup time as the recovery floor; every switchover is an event either way.

### [SERIOUS] If the W2 bake-off picks libpeer (option b), the W3 degraded ladder's middle rung disappears: libpeer's TURN client allocates over plain UDP sockets only (no TURN-over-TCP/TLS transport exists in the tree), and the SFU's only documented non-UDP media path is TURN — so on a UDP-blocked network a libpeer device cannot reach the SFU at all and the ladder collapses to 'UDP blocked → WS PCM fallback'. The plan records 'TURN-over-UDP only' as a libpeer attribute in W2 but presents 'UDP blocked → TURN-over-TLS:443 → WS PCM' in W3 as a lane property independent of the bake-off outcome.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:386-392 (W2 libpeer 'TURN-over-UDP only'), PLAN.md:394-396 (W3 ladder unconditional); ~/src/github.com/sepfy/libpeer/src/agent.c:181-236 (TURN allocation via udp_socket_sendto; no TCP/TLS TURN path in src/); ~/src/github.com/espressif/esp-webrtc-solution/components/esp_peer/include/esp_peer_default.h:80-83 (TURNS supported in the blob option)
- Proposed fix: Make TURN-over-TLS reachability an explicit W2 bake-off criterion with a decision rule: choosing libpeer means either (i) writing/porting a TURN-TCP/TLS transport for it (scope that work honestly), or (ii) accepting that hostile-network devices skip WebRTC entirely and live on the WS PCM lane — and if (ii), W3's ladder text becomes conditional on the chosen stack. Record which rung each candidate actually has in the W2 results table.

### [MINOR] The signaling paragraph ('our worker does the SDP exchange on its behalf over the existing control lane') understates what the control lane must carry: pulling the downlink track is an SFU-initiated offer/answer (renegotiation) round, and every ingest-adapter recreate repeats it — so renegotiation traffic is event-driven at conversation starts and adapter deaths, not a one-shot session-start exchange. Not periodic (nothing in the docs requires periodic renegotiation), but the control-lane protocol needs message types for it.

### [MINOR] The adapter is beta with 'the API may change' and an undocumented auth surface (no custom headers; the wss endpoint secures itself — capability-URL token only, which matches the plan) and a protobuf schema quoted only in docs (fields 1, 2, 5 with unexplained gaps). ## lens

**Overall:** The track's architecture-level claims held up under adversarial verification — the SFU WebSocket adapter is real and shaped as described, xAI is WebSocket-only, and the WS-fallback posture is sound — but the W1-W4 execution text has one blocker and a cluster of serious sequencing and instrumentation holes. The blocker: W4 gates migration on the existing proof ladder, and I empirically showed the PRBS31 oracle cannot pass through Opus at its current 0.8 soft-correlation threshold (0-6.5% of chips survive at 32-64 kbps), while Opus PLC simultaneously blinds the tone rung to real loss — the plan's acceptance instruments were designed for a transparent PCM path and no W-item recalibrates them. Sequencing: W1 secretly depends on (or must front-load) stage 4's D8 split because the deployed PcmSessionBridge welds provider lifetime to a single device socket, W3 depends on stage 2's capture move and stage 3's profile struct, and the done-list's W2 requires a second device-touching window the codex-quiet discipline doesn't budget. The two-transport reality also isn't paid for in §3 (single-lane scenario list, thresholds, nightly), and two verified factual errors need fixing: 'RTP timestamps native' does not cover the downlink-playout half that server-side AEC needs (ingest adapter accepts payload only), and 'possibly zero resampling' conflicts with mid-call WS fallback and every 16 kHz-assuming instrument. All fixable as plan-text edits before execution; none reopens the product decision.

### [BLOCKER] W4's migration gate — 'the WebRTC lane must pass the full proof ladder (tone → PRBS → endurance)' — is empirically unsatisfiable as written: the PRBS31 oracle's per-chip soft-correlation threshold (0.8) cannot be met through the Opus leg even on a perfect network. I synthesized the oracle's signal shape (dual carriers 1 kHz/2 kHz, 1 ms chips, PRBS31, 16 kHz PCM) and round-tripped it through libopus (voip application): at 32 kbps only 6.5%/3.2% of chips (per carrier) reach 0.8 (mean soft corr ≈0.70, p01 ≈0.5); at 64 kbps via the 48 kHz SFU-decode path, 0.0% of chips reach 0.8. The plan never schedules verifying that its own acceptance instruments survive a lossy codec. Second-order effect: Opus PLC conceals genuine loss, so the tone rung's continuity check can PASS during real outages — the instruments' semantics invert on this lane, right where W3's churn scenarios need them most.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:399-401 (W4 gate); apps/kit/src/device/acoustic-prbs31-challenge.ts:11 (defaultMinimumSoftCorrelation = 0.8) and :526 (per-chip rejection); experiment at scratchpad/prbs_opus_test.py — libopus 32k direct-16k: frac≥0.8 = 0.065/0.032; 64k via 48k: 0.000/0.000
- Proposed fix: Add an explicit W1 deliverable, 'oracle equivalence through the codec': render the real DualCarrierPrbs31Renderer fixture, run it through the actual SFU loop (host WebRTC client), and either (a) re-derive lane-specific thresholds/integration windows (e.g. multi-chip coherent integration, lower chip rate) or (b) design a codec-tolerant variant that combines RTP sequence continuity with acoustic-energy/timeline checks. Reword W4 to 'lane-calibrated instruments asserting the same physical outcomes as the WS-lane ladder', and give the §3 thresholds module a per-lane dimension. Make this a hard prerequisite of W4 — otherwise the gate will be quietly gamed by ad-hoc threshold lowering.

### [SERIOUS] W1 ('server half, zero firmware, start now') hides a dependency on stage 4's D8 worker split. The deployed proxy welds provider lifetime to the device socket: PcmSessionBridge takes exactly one device WS + one provider WS and device-close tears down the whole session. The SFU adapter is two unidirectional connections with asymmetric direction (egress: SFU dials US; ingest: WE dial the SFU — verified live against CF docs; the exploration doc never states the ingest direction), protobuf-framed, and the egress adapter CLOSES PERMANENTLY after 5 s of failed reconnect ('your application must create a new adapter'). Bolting this onto the welded bridge means every adapter blip kills the Grok session; doing it right means building the device-connection/provider-session seam — i.e., part of stage 4 — before or inside W1. Otherwise W1's latency and beta-edge measurements are taken on throwaway plumbing and don't transfer.

- Evidence: apps/kit/src/userspace/config-worker/pcm-proxy.ts:68 (PcmSessionBridge), :111-121 (device close → this.close(1000, "Device socket closed.")); PLAN.md:299-300 (D8 split scheduled in stage 4) vs PLAN.md:375 (W1 'start now'); developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/media-transport-adapters/websocket-adapter/ (ingest: 'You connect to Cloudflare's WebSocket endpoint'; egress close-after-5s)
- Proposed fix: Reorder explicitly: make W1 the first consumer of the D8 split — pull 'device-connection / provider-session split' out of stage 4 into a named W1 prerequisite (the adapter pair becomes the first alternate 'device connection'). Expand W1's scope line to include adapter lifecycle management: create/teardown via the HTTPS API, both directions, re-create-on-permanent-close, and the outbound-ingest client owned by the DO (note: an outbound WebSocket pins the DO — no hibernation).

### [SERIOUS] W1's 'xAI accepts 48 kHz natively so possibly zero resampling' conflicts with the track's own fallback design and with every existing observability instrument. If the xAI session opens at 48 kHz for the WebRTC lane, W3's mid-call fallback to the WS PCM lane (16 kHz mono, 640-byte frames — validated at the proxy) lands a sample-rate mismatch mid-provider-session. Meanwhile PcmSessionMetrics, the acoustic ledger, the recorder, and both oracles all assume 16 k/640-byte frames — on the WebRTC lane they'd see 48 kHz stereo protobuf, so 'observability unchanged' (the exploration doc's claim) is only true after conversion work the plan never names. The adapter is also stereo both directions and ingest accepts payload-only protobuf — channel down/upmix and the protobuf codec are mandatory code regardless.

### [SERIOUS] Requirement-map row 9's 'WebRTC lane: RTP timestamps native' is wrong for the half that matters. D7's timestamp echo is a CROSS-binding: the device tags each MIC frame with the timestamp of the SPEAKER audio playing at that moment — that is what server-side AEC needs. RTP gives uplink capture timestamps only. Downlink playout timing does not exist on this lane: the ingest adapter uses only the protobuf payload field (no timestamps accepted — verified live), the SFU paces its own buffer, and the device's Opus jitter buffer adds unobservable delay. The server cannot know when a given downlink sample played. Server-side AEC on the WebRTC lane is strictly HARDER than on the WS lane, and no W-item builds the missing mechanism.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:480 (row 9), PLAN.md:396-398 (W3 'RTP gives us D7's sequence+timestamp machinery natively'), PLAN.md:168-172 (D7's definition of timestamp echo); CF adapter docs: ingest 'Only the payload field is used'
- Proposed fix: Correct row 9 and W3's text: RTP covers uplink timing only. Add a W3 work item re-homing D7's timestamp echo for this lane — the device periodically reports 'RTP timestamp of the sample now leaving the speaker' over the control lane (or a data channel), and the W3 timestamp-alignment rig scenario asserts that report against the PRBS/tone ground truth. Without it, req 9 is silently undischarged on the target transport.

### [SERIOUS] Split-brain health is unhandled: W3's degraded ladder (UDP blocked → TURN/TLS:443 → WS PCM fallback) covers only device-side connectivity. The WebRTC lane has three independent segments (device↔SFU RTP, SFU↔DO adapter ×2 directions, DO↔xAI). Requirement 10 ('keep connections at all times') is discharged by the ladder in the requirement map, but the ladder can't see this failure class.

Add the adapter-leg row(s) to the degraded matrix.

## lens

**Overall:** The Transport track's server half (W1) is fundamentally sound — the SFU-as-WebSocket-client-of-/pcm shape is verified and preserves D8/req 11 — but the device half is written as if WebRTC were a config change when the plan's own evidence base shows it is not. Two blockers: the DIRAM arithmetic already fails (109 KB of Opus/WebRTC task stacks against 77.8 KiB of free internal heap, before DTLS, codec state, event/SD additions, or StackChan's 31-60 KB internal AEC reservation — PSRAM residency is mandatory, not a W2 measurement outcome), and PTT commit/turn boundaries have no mapping onto RTP at all, silently reintroducing the cross-socket commit race D7 exists to kill, with no tail-of-utterance delivery guarantee on either candidate stack. Three claims in the track text are factually wrong: the playback ring is not a reorderer (and libpeer strips sequence numbers before the app ever sees them), 'RTP timestamps native' does not discharge requirement 9's mic-speaker alignment (the ingest leg carries no timestamps), and the W4 gate's PRBS31 chip-exact oracle cannot pass through a lossy Opus transcode, making the promotion gate unmeetable as written. The W2 bake-off criteria (DIRAM/CPU/battery) omit the axes that will actually decide the winner: TURN-over-TLS support (libpeer lacks it, contradicting W3's ladder), failure-detection latency (10-30 s defaults vs today's 200 ms), per-packet observability (the blob exposes none, starving the §8 promotion-trigger telemetry), and task placement against the core-1 audio invariant. Each hole has a concrete plan-text fix above; none of them argues against the WebRTC decision itself.

### [BLOCKER] The DIRAM ledger cannot close in internal RAM and the track defers to W2 measurement what arithmetic already decides: Opus enc (40 KB) + dec (40 KB) task stacks + peer loop (25 KB) + send task (4 KB) = 109 KB of stacks alone — Espressif's own demo ground truth — against the Stick's ~77.8 KiB total free internal heap, before Opus codec state (~30 KB), DTLS context, SRTP, reorder storage, or the D4 event rings / SD 4 KiB CRC blocks. On StackChan, add the 31–60 KB FD_LOW_COST AEC reservation that esp-sr requires to be internal. W2's parenthetical '(Opus ~40 KB task stacks; DIRAM ledger entry mandatory)' understates by ~3x, and the risk line 'DIRAM pressure when Opus+WebRTC meet StackChan's AEC budget — measured in W2' pretends measurement can change the conclusion. PSRAM residency for essentially the whole WebRTC/Opus footprint is mandatory on both boards, which makes stage-1 PSRAM-at-80MHz a hard prerequisite of W2 (the track only says it 'runs beside the numbered stages'), requires a named PSRAM-task-stack policy (SPIRAM_ALLOW_STACK_EXTERNAL_MEMORY constraints, cache-miss jitter on the realtime encode path), and puts Opus/WebRTC working-set traffic on the same octal PSRAM bus as AEC model fetches — the exact contention the plan's own XIP analysis warns about.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:387-390 (W2), PLAN.md:403-406 (risks), PLAN.md:157-161 (D6 budget: 142,465 B static / ~77.8 KiB heap), exploration/webrtc-esp-side.md §2 task table (~155 KB stacks, 'For OPUS encoder it need huge stack'), exploration/contention-knobs.md §1.2 and §3 (PSRAM bus contention)
- Proposed fix: Write the ledger arithmetic into the Transport track text now. Amend W2: (i) stage-1 PSRAM enable + SPIRAM_SPEED_80M is a stated prerequisite, not a parallel chore; (ii) default hypothesis to disprove = 'all Opus/WebRTC stacks and state PSRAM-resident; internal RAM reserved for the existing audio path + AEC'; (iii) add a PSRAM-stack decision row (xTaskCreateStatic with SPIRAM stack vs shrunk stacks vs heap_caps-redirected Opus state) each gated on the tone/PRBS jitter rig; (iv) add perfmon-based PSRAM/cache contention measurement (Opus+DTLS running while AEC-sized PSRAM streaming is simulated) to W2's criteria, not just 'DIRAM/PSRAM cost'.

### [BLOCKER] PTT commit/turn boundaries are unmapped on the WebRTC lane — the track contains zero text on them. Wire v1's commit is an empty WebSocket message and D7 exists precisely to move end-of-turn IN-BAND on the media path because 'the PTT release races the last audio frames up a different socket'. RTP has no end-of-stream (marker bit = talkspurt start; stopping transmission is indistinguishable from loss at the SFU adapter), so on the WebRTC lane the commit reverts to the control lane racing the media tail through a DIFFERENT transport (UDP + SFU + adapter backlog vs TCP) — the exact race class D7 closes, reintroduced worse. And with libpeer there is no NACK/retransmission, so the tail of the utterance can be silently lost with no delivery confirmation: the peer-delivery guard's 40 ms barrier / 200 ms replace ('PTT release must not lose the last words') has no equivalent, and neither W2's bake-off criteria nor W3's rig scenarios measure tail-of-utterance delivery.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:163-175 (D7, esp. lines 171-174), PLAN.md:20-22 (empty-message end-of-stream), PLAN.md:391-398 (W3 — no turn-boundary work), exploration/webrtc-esp-side.md §6e (peer-delivery guard has no RTCP equivalent), ~/src/github.com/sepfy/libpeer/src/rtcp.c (58 lines, no retransmission)
- Proposed fix: Add an explicit W3 work item: the control-lane commit message carries the final RTP timestamp + sequence number of the utterance; the DO holds turn-commit until the adapter's stream-mode Packet.timestamp/sequenceNumber reaches that mark, or a bounded timeout declares tail loss and emits an event. Add a W3 rig scenario 'PTT tail delivery under induced uplink loss' and make it a named W4 gate row alongside tone/PRBS/endurance.

### [SERIOUS] Requirement-map row 9's 'WebRTC lane: RTP timestamps native' is a false discharge for server-side AEC. D7's timestamp echo is the device tagging each MIC frame with the timestamp of the SPEAKER audio playing at that instant — a device-side correlation. On the WebRTC lane the DO sees only the adapter's uplink seq/timestamps (which date capture, not playback); the downlink DO→ingest-adapter leg carries NO timestamps at all (ingest Packets use only the payload field — verified against the adapter doc), the SFU adds unmeasured latency, and neither candidate device stack exposes RTP header extensions to echo playback position. There is no server-observable mic↔speaker alignment on this lane; the groundwork for server-side echo cancellation does NOT come free with RTP.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:479 (row 9), PLAN.md:395-398 (W3 'RTP gives us D7's sequence+timestamp machinery natively'), https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/media-transport-adapters/websocket-adapter/ (ingest mode: only payload field used), exploration/webrtc-esp-side.md §3 (blob exposes only pts/data/size)
- Proposed fix: Amend row 9 and W3: RTP supplies uplink sequencing to the DO but not mic↔speaker alignment. Either (a) keep a timestamp-echo equivalent on the WebRTC lane — device reports 'playback RTP timestamp currently at the DAC' at low rate over the control lane (or a data channel) — or (b) explicitly scope server-side AEC groundwork to the WS lane and accept speak-state gating only on WebRTC. Re-scope the W3 'timestamp-alignment' rig scenario to whichever is chosen.

### [SERIOUS] W4's promotion gate — 'must pass the full proof ladder (tone → PRBS → endurance)' — is not passable by a lossy transcoded lane as written. The PRBS31 oracle is a chip-exact skip/dup/drift decode (anchors every 1,000 chips, zero skip/dup gates) designed for a bit-transparent PCM path. On the WebRTC lane the downlink is 16k→48k-upsampled PCM, Opus-encoded by the SFU, decoded at 16 kHz on-device: a noise-like PRBS waveform is exactly what Opus vector-quantizes worst, so chip decode degrades or fails regardless of transport health. The gate is either unmeetable (the lane never promotes) or will be quietly weakened ad hoc during W4.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:399-402 (W4), apps/kit/docs/fable-acoustic-oracle-and-endurance-review-2026-07-30.md:440,674 (PRBS31 chip anchors, zero skip/dup promotion), adapter doc (48 kHz Opus transcode both directions)
- Proposed fix: Define the codec-aware ladder in W4 now: tone-coherence rung unchanged (sine survives Opus); replace the PRBS rung on this lane with adapter seq/timestamp continuity + device decode-loss/PLC counters (as generated metric fields) + word-level transcription assertions from the rig speech toolset; state explicitly that byte-identity/chip-exact oracles are WS-lane-only. If a PRBS-class signal is still wanted, specify a band-limited variant with re-derived thresholds as a W1 host experiment before hardware exists.

### [SERIOUS] W2's claim 'we bring our own receive reordering, which our proven playback ring already is' is wrong twice. (a) The playback ring is an in-order 4-descriptor/80 ms FIFO fed by TCP — it has no sequence-indexed insertion or reordering. (b) libpeer's generic RTP receive path strips the RTP header and delivers payload-only to onaudiotrack, so the firmware never sees sequence numbers — it can neither reorder nor detect loss to invoke Opus PLC (opus_decode(NULL,...)) without patching libpeer. The track also ignores codec statefulness vs the freshness policy: frames dropped by the 200 ms freshness cap must still advance the Opus decoder (decode-then-discard) or playback artifacts result. Downlink loss handling — the lane's raison d'être — is entirely unbudgeted code.

- Evidence: PLAN.md:384-387 (W2b), exploration/judge-realtime.md:267 (4-descriptor/80 ms window), ~/src/github.com/sepfy/libpeer/src/rtp.c:271-277 (rtp_decode_generic passes payload only), ~/src/github.com/sepfy/libpeer/src/peer_connection.h:79 (onaudiotrack signature: data+size only)
- Proposed fix: Rewrite the W2(b) parenthetical: 'we bring our own depacketizer: patch libpeer to surface RTP seq/timestamp, insert into a seq-indexed ring ahead of the playback FIFO, invoke Opus PLC on gaps, decode-and-discard frames the freshness policy rejects'. Add downlink-loss PLC quality and reorder handling to the W3 churn scenario's measured outputs, and count the depacketizer + PLC-invocation code in the W2 firmware estimate.

### [SERIOUS] The bake-off criteria and the degraded ladder contradict each other, and W2 measures the wrong things for requirement 10. W3's ladder is 'UDP blocked → TURN-over-TLS:443 → WS PCM', but option (b) libpeer supports TURN over UDP only — if UDP is blocked, libpeer's middle rung does not exist, so ladder support must be a bake-off criterion and it currently isn't. Failure-detection latency is also unmeasured: esp_peer defaults to ~30 s peer-death detection (6 s STUN keepalive x 5) and libpeer's CONFIG_KEEPALIVE_TIMEOUT is 10 s, vs the current lane's 200 ms replace — 'keep connections at all times' (req 10) needs a stated detection/transition time budget per rung, including ICE restart after a Wi-Fi roam (new IP/port = full re-signaling through the worker). Finally, observability: the esp_peer blob exposes no seq/RTCP surface, so W3's 'measure loss-concealment' and §8 item 16's promotion-trigger telemetry cannot be collected on path (a) at all; and neither candidate's default task placement conforms to the core/priority invariant (the demo pins pc_task prio 18 and the 40 KB Opus encoder on core 1 next to the prio-19 audio task).

- Evidence: PLAN.md:388-394 (W2 criteria + W3 ladder), exploration/webrtc-esp-side.md §3-4 (TURN capabilities, ~30 s detection, blob hides seq/RTCP; demo task table), ~/src/github.com/sepfy/libpeer/src/config.h:52-53 (10 s keepalive), ~/src/github.com/sepfy/libpeer/src/agent.c:195-233 (UDP-only relay), exploration/contention-knobs.md §4 invariant 5

### [SERIOUS] DTLS-SRTP's cost on top of the existing TLS stack is never itemized. DTLS is not even compiled in v1 (CONFIG_MBEDTLS_SSL_PROTO_DTLS is not set); enabling it adds flash plus a third concurrent mbedTLS context (control WSS + fallback PCM WSS + DTLS), and mbedTLS record buffers are a GLOBAL config (IN=16384/OUT=4096, no dynamic buffers) so the DTLS context inherits the 16 KB IN buffer unless buffer strategy work is done; the TURN-over-TLS rung adds a FOURTH TLS context while falling back. WebRTC also requires a per-session self-signed certificate — ECDSA keygen + DTLS handshake on a 160 MHz S3 directly sets session-setup and reconnect latency and is nowhere measured.

- Evidence: apps/kit/firmware/targets/m5sticks3/sdkconfig:1732-1734,1804 (IN/OUT content len, no dynamic buffer, DTLS off), PLAN.md:381-390 (W2 mentions none of this), exploration/webrtc-esp-side.md §1.1 (dtls_srtp.c over mbedTLS, CONFIG_MBEDTLS_SSL_DTLS_SRTP needed)
- Proposed fix: Add to W2's DIRAM ledger and measurement list: (i) flash + heap delta of enabling MBEDTLS_SSL_PROTO_DTLS + MBEDTLS_SSL_DTLS_SRTP under the real global buffer config, and whether MBEDTLS_DYNAMIC_BUFFER or reduced content lengths are needed; (ii) maximum concurrent TLS/DTLS context count in each ladder state, with heap at that point; (iii) cert-generation + DTLS handshake wall time on the Stick at 160 and 240 MHz, feeding the W3 rung time budgets.

### [SERIOUS] W1 under-specifies the adapter integration in ways that leak stale audio and miss failure modes: (a) each direction is a separate unidirectional adapter — two SFU-dialed-out sockets to the DO with independent lifecycles; (b) auto-reconnect (5 s, best-effort, bounded backlog, not exactly-once) applies to STREAM mode only — an ingest-side socket death has no documented recovery, so the worker must detect and recreate via the HTTPS API; (c) format is fixed 48 kHz STEREO s16le in both directions, so 'possibly zero resampling' is only half-true (stereo downmix/interleave always required; DO ingests 192 KB/s per direction, 6x today's 32 KB/s, in protobuf — DO CPU/duration cost unmeasured); (d) stream-mode duplicates require seq-based dedup in the DO; (e) the reconnect backlog can replay seconds-stale audio after a hiccup — the DO must extend the freshness policy to the adapter leg (drop by adapter timestamp) or stale voice reaches xAI and the device. - Evidence: https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/media-transport-adapters/websocket-adapter/ (fetched 2026-07-31: dial-out both modes, ingest payload-only, stream-only reconnect, 48 kHz stereo both directions, 32 KB max message), PLAN.md:375-380 (W1)
- Proposed fix: Expand W1's deliverables: dual-adapter lifecycle state machine with ingest-death detection + recreate; seq dedup + adapter-leg staleness drop with a stated threshold; DO-side CPU/duration measurement at 192 KB/s per direction; and a loss/churn injection run (netem on the host stand-in) characterizing what the adapter emits under RTP loss (gaps vs SFU-side PLC vs duplicate frames) — the DO's pacing toward xAI depends on the answer.
